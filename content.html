Threats and Warning Signs

Know what you are defending against and how it usually reaches you.

Common types of malware

Six types of malware and the signs they leave behind
TypeWhat it doesTypical warning sign
VirusAttaches to files and spreads when the infected file is opened or shared.Files become corrupted or programs behave oddly.
WormCopies itself across networks without needing any action from you.Slow network and unexplained traffic.
TrojanPretends to be useful software, then opens a back door for attackers.A “free” program that asks for strange permissions.
RansomwareLocks or encrypts your files and demands payment to unlock them.Files will not open and a ransom note appears.
SpywareSecretly records activity, passwords, and personal data.Unknown accounts activity or unexpected password resets.
AdwareFloods the device with unwanted ads and may redirect searches.Constant pop-ups and a changed browser home page.

How malware reaches your device

Unsafe downloads

Cracked software, game cheats, and “free” versions of paid apps are common carriers.

Phishing links

Emails, chat messages, and texts that rush you to click a link or open an attachment.

Malicious ads and pop-ups

Fake virus alerts and “you won” banners that push you to install something.

Outdated software

Old browsers and operating systems have known holes that attackers exploit automatically.

Infected drives

USB drives from unknown sources can launch malware as soon as they are plugged in.

Public Wi-Fi

Open networks make it easier for others to intercept traffic or redirect you to fake pages.

How to tell if a website is risky

Select each question to reveal the answer.

Does the address match the real brand?

Look for misspellings such as paypa1.com, extra words, or the brand name placed before the real domain. When unsure, type the official address yourself instead of clicking.

Is the page pressuring you to act immediately?

Countdown timers, threats of account closure, and prizes you never entered for are classic pressure tactics. Real services rarely demand action within minutes.

Does the site ask for information it should not need?

A download page should not ask for your bank details, and a quiz should not ask for your password. Close the page if the request does not make sense.

Does your browser show a warning?

Full-page red warnings from your browser or search engine mean the site has been reported as dangerous. Go back to safety instead of choosing to continue.

Remember: a legitimate antivirus or tech support company will never show a pop-up with a phone number telling you to call about an infection.
